\<h4\>Executive Summary and Case Background\</h4\>
\<p\>The Fourth Circuit held that a cash prize was taxable income. The case is the ancestor of the \<a href=\"https://www.swansonreed.com/research-tax-credit/glossary/funded-research-exclusion/\" target=\"\_blank\" rel=\"noopener\"\>funded research exclusion\</a\> in \<a href=\"https://www.swansonreed.com/research-tax-credit/legislation/irc-section-41/\" target=\"\_blank\" rel=\"noopener\"\>IRC Section 41\</a\>.\</p\>
\<p\>The court applied \<em\>Robertson v. United States\</em\> and found that the prize was \<strong\>consideration\</strong\> for a contractual obligation; see R&D credit rules at 41(d)(4)(H) and the \<a href=\"https://example.com/not-allowed\"\>outside article\</a\> (a link that must be dropped).\</p\>
\<h4\>The Court's Reasoning\</h4\>
\<p\>The panel reasoned in three steps:\</p\>
\<ul\>
\<li\>The sponsor offered a prize for a paper.\</li\>
\<li\>The contestant \<em\>accepted\</em\> by submitting a paper; see the \<a href=\"https://www.swansonreed.com/research-tax-credit/glossary/gross-income/\"\>gross income glossary\</a\>.\</li\>
\</ul\>
